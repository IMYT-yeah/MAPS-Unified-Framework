## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation and background

The two pillars of modern physics — general relativity (GR) and quantum field theory (QFT) — precisely describe gravity and the other three fundamental forces respectively, yet their unification (quantum gravity) is the deepest unsolved problem of physics. The Standard Model unifies strong, weak and electromagnetic forces via the SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) gauge group; GR describes gravity via Riemannian geometry. However:

- Gravity is non-renormalizable and lacks a complete quantum description;
- The Standard Model contains 19–26 free parameters (hierarchy problem, cosmological-constant problem, strong-CP problem);
- The relative strengths of the four forces (1 : 10⁻² : 10⁻⁶ : 10⁻³⁸) lack a unified explanation;
- Candidate paths such as grand unification (GUT), string theory, and loop quantum gravity have all lacked experimental support.

This framework proposes a different unification strategy: **instead of postulating a larger symmetry group or extra dimensions, it seeks a single underlying statistical mechanism (the event flow) such that the four forces become emergent modes of the same mechanism at different scales/configurations**. This strategy is analogous to the relation of statistical mechanics to thermodynamics: GR is the geometric description, and the framework's statistical-fluid description is its effective theory.
